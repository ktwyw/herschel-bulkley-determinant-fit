How the method works

For data (xᵢ, yᵢ) and the model y = y₀ + K xⁿ, the least-squares normal equations ∂S/∂y₀ = ∂S/∂K = ∂S/∂n = 0 are three equations that are linear in y₀, K and a constant, with coefficients that depend only on n. They have a non-trivial solution only when the determinant of the coefficients vanishes (paper Eq. 5):

mΣxⁿΣxⁿ ln xΣxⁿΣx²ⁿΣx²ⁿ ln xΣyΣxⁿyΣxⁿy ln x = F(n) = 0.

  1. Scale the data by X = max x and Y = max y (Eq. 6): the roots do not move and the determinant stays well conditioned.
  2. Find the root of F̄(n) by bracketing sign changes on a grid and bisection — no derivatives, no starting guess.
  3. With n known, y₀ and K follow from a 2 × 2 linear least-squares solve.

Because the consistency condition is exactly the stationarity condition of S, the result is the same least-squares minimum a Gauss–Newton or Levenberg–Marquardt solver converges to — the app verifies this by running a few Levenberg–Marquardt steps from the solution and reporting how far it moves (it should not). Negative K is allowed, so decaying curves such as the paper's chlorine-transport example (y = a − ab xⁿ) fit as well.
